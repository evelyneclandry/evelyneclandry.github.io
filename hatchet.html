Brian Robeson

Glad To Be Home

13-year-old Brian Robeson has been found alive after surviving 54 days alone in the Canadian wilderness following a bush plane crash.

Brian was flying from a small airport in New York heading to Canada to visit his father. The miniscule plane only had him and the pilot as passengers on board. A few hours after takeoff, the pilot was burping and farting before he died of a heart attack. With no one else on board, Brian had to fly the plane. He was scared that he would have to land the plane by himself.

Before dying, the pilot had taught Brian the basics of flying planes, so Brian was able to take control with a little bit of confidence. The pilot had showed him how to steer and keep the plane going forward. When he noticed the plane was low on fuel, Brian had no choice but to crash land the plane into an L-shaped lake. While flying downwards, Brian was so scared he could barely move. When the plane hit the water, there was a bit of an impact, and the plane started to sink. He tried to kick the door open, but it would not move. He had to wait until the plane was submerged. He escaped the sinking plane with his clothes and a hatchet that his mother gave him before he left.

While trying to swim to exit the plane, he hurt his leg on a sharp corner. It was a bad scrape that made it very painful to swim to shore. When he got to shore, his leg was hurting and bloody. He was also very thirsty. Brian drank a lot of lake water which made him sick and he threw up. Luckily, he only threw up once.

Brian knew he would have to make shelter to be safe. After a bit of looking in the forest, he found a perfectly shaped rock that looked genuinely like a nice shelter. So, he set up his broken windbreaker, his hatchet, and his money in there.

Brian needed to find food soon or he would starve. Brian was so hungry he had to do something that would get his mind off food. He left his shelter to search in the forest for berries, and other edible things. He stepped on a bird called a fool bird that scared him, but remembered birds have meat and he could maybe try to catch one of those birds to eat. He also found some turtle eggs, and after eating a few turtle eggs he got used to them and really liked them. He was feeling better because he wasn’t as hungry anymore. He was happy that he found lots of berries, too. He eventually caught fish, rabbits, and one of the fool birds.

As the days went on, he had to find out how to store the fish because it went bad and attracted animals. For example, a skunk got into his shelter and sprayed him. He had to improve his shelter, so he spent a whole week making it better.

Brian was thinking that a fire would be a good help to keep mosquitoes away and keep him warm, but he couldn’t do it. After throwing his hatchet against the wall in anger, Brian saw that it made sparks. This reminded him that he had a tiny $20 bill, which is made of paper and is exceptionally good for fires. He tried again and made more sparks and used some on the $20 bill to light it on fire. He put the flaming $20 bill in the fire pit filled with the dry wood. The fire started small, but Brian added more wood, and it made a noticeably big fire.

He encountered a lot of animals on his journey, like a skunk, a porcupine, a bear, wolves, and even a cow, which is a female moose. He encountered also different types of birds like the fool birds, cardinals and robins.

Many weeks after the plane crash, a tornado passed. It revealed part of the crashed plane. Brian remembered that there were an emergency transmitter and a survival pack in there. Once he retrieved the transmitter with the survival pack, he was able to contact a pilot, which a few days later came to rescue him.

After many days of people trying to find Brian, using helicopters and planes but not finding any clues to where he might be, his parents thought he was dead. The L shaped lake where Brian crashed was too small for them to see. His parents were incredibly sad, and they held a funeral for him and everything.

It might have been day 45, and Brian was sleeping in his shelter. A tornado passed through the L shaped lake. All of Brian’s things blew away, and his shelter broke. With his things gone, Brian felt disappointed. Feeling very hopeless, Brian hurt himself.

He didn’t die, and he decided to try and find his tools. He found a few of them, and then because of the tornado, he was able to see the plane. The reflection of the metal from the plane was clearer now to see. The plane was in the lake, and he swam out to it. He found the survival kit. This gave him a bit of hope, and he swam back to the shore. He tried the emergency transmitter in hopes someone might find him.

There were people still searching for him. A searcher spotted Brian’s plane in the lake. On day 54, when one searcher found Brian alive, they were all very, very happy. They brought Brian to his parents, and they were so thankful to have him back.

“I’m so glad the tornado passed.” said Brian. “It revealed part of the plane which led me to retrieve the transmitter and the survival pack.”
“I was lucky to have my transmitter turned on to rescue Brian before the frosty winter set in.” the rescue pilot said. “I was very impressed that Brian was able to survive 54 days (about 2 months) in the Canadian wilderness by eating fish, turtle eggs, berries, and a few fool birds. Fool birds are a type of bird.”

This reporter thinks that Brian faced lots of challenges. He was up against a female moose that hurt his rib unbelievably bad, and he was able to start a fire with only a hatchet and a $20 bill. I am happy that he is home with his parents.

I think that Brian will want to return to living a normal life. He missed his mom’s roast and chocolate bars the most. He’s also looking forward to having some more time biking around and playing games with his friends. Believe it or not, Brian now has dreams of becoming a pilot.

Brian learned a lot in the wilderness; how to make a few hunting tools, like a bow and arrow and a spear, to make shelter and to make a fire. “I was incredibly happy to have a fire that helped me cook and keep insects away.” Brian said.

Small mistakes can result in serious problems. He is incredibly happy that he had a fire. The fire will help him cook and keep insects away. As the reporter, it's been a pleasure to report on such a brave young man. This story is quite an adventure that I'm sure he hopes not to repeat the same challenges that he went through.
